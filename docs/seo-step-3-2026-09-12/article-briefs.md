## K101 — 4 month old sleep schedule

**Reader’s task:** Understand how daytime naps and overnight sleep can fit together at four months, without treating one timetable as a developmental test.

**Proposed title:** 4 Month Old Sleep Schedule: Building a Flexible Day  
**Proposed route:** `baby-and-child/4-month-old-sleep-schedule` (not created)  
**Primary phrase:** 4 month old sleep schedule  
**Related editorial questions:** four-month naps, bedtime, short naps, night waking, tracking a day of sleep. These related phrases are writing hypotheses, not newly verified Keyword Planner results.

### Evidence and overlap

[Huckleberry’s four-month sleep page](https://huckleberrycare.com/blog/4-month-olds-and-sleep) was checked September 12; its displayed update was August 27, 2026. It addresses an example day, naps, bedtime and common sleep questions. Its precise wake-window and nap targets should not be presented as universal clinical standards.

| --- | --- | --- |

| [Sleep regressions](https://bemamas.com/baby-and-child/sleep-regressions/) | Changes in previously established sleep | How to adjust an ordinary day without labeling every disruption a regression |
| [Newborn sleep](https://bemamas.com/newborn/newborn-sleep/) | Sleep in the first months | Four-month context; no need for another generic newborn guide |
| [Sleep training](https://bemamas.com/baby-and-child/sleep-training/) | Approaches families may choose | Planning a day does not require choosing a sleep-training method |

Existing overlap is real. The new page earns its own URL only by answering the schedule task in depth. Before adding cross-links, review the existing regression page’s fixed duration and age claims, and the sleep-training title’s outcome claim. Do not repeat them simply because they are already on BeMama.

### Original outline and useful visual

1. **Answer first: a flexible rhythm at four months.** Explain the difference between total sleep guidance and a sample clock-time schedule. No promise of sleeping through the night.
2. **What the daily total includes.** Show the broad recommended sleep range with age scope and a source beside it. Explain that an individual baby’s needs require context.
3. **Read yesterday before planning today.** Separate recorded daytime sleep, overnight sleep, waking and missing entries. Include a short fictional log with its arithmetic shown.
4. **An example day you can adapt.** Create one original, clearly labeled illustration. Show sleep blocks on a 24-hour timeline, with a matching accessible table. Identify all times as an example; do not label it ideal, prescribed or clinically validated.
5. **When the day changes.** Explain how to think about short naps, a later morning or disrupted sleep. Do not prescribe minute-specific wake windows without a separate evidence review.
6. **Keep the sleep setting safe.** A concise sourced box, separate from scheduling advice.
7. **What to record and what to ask.** Link to BeMama’s [baby sleep tracking workflow](https://bemamas.com/about-bemama/tools/), including its missing-data limitation. Give a place for questions for the baby’s clinician, not an automated diagnosis.
8. **FAQ.** Does a short nap mean the day is wrong? Does tracking sleep predict the next nap? Is a sample schedule a reason to delay a hungry baby’s feed? Answers must respect feeding needs and individualized care.

**Visual specification:** Use the existing BeMama type, sage palette, cards and spacing. Draw the timeline in HTML/SVG from the same example data used by the table; include labels and a legend that do not depend on color. Provide stacked mobile rows and print styling. Do not use an AI-generated chart: chart values and labels must be exact. If a photo is needed, use an appropriate existing asset without depicting an unsafe sleep environment.

## K102 — 6 month old feeding schedule

**Reader’s task:** Fit early solid-food experiences around milk feeds and hunger/fullness cues, without confusing an example routine with a prescribed intake.

**Proposed title:** 6 Month Old Feeding Schedule: Milk and First Foods  
**Proposed route:** `baby-and-child/6-month-old-feeding-schedule` (not created)  
**Primary phrase:** 6 month old feeding schedule  
**Related editorial questions:** milk and solids at six months, early meal routine, feeding cues, breast milk and formula records. Related phrases require their own demand validation if used to justify further articles.

### Evidence and overlap

[Huckleberry’s six-month feeding page](https://huckleberrycare.com/blog/6-month-old-feeding-schedule) was checked September 12; its displayed update was September 3, 2026. It contains example feeding timetables, amounts, feeding challenges and FAQs. Those tables and amounts are not a dataset to copy into BeMama or a substitute for primary-source review.

| Existing BeMama page | Keep its purpose | What the new article contributes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Starting solids](https://bemamas.com/baby-and-child/starting-solids/) | Readiness, first foods, textures and broad safety | How to plan the day when milk and first foods coexist |
| [Starting solids and allergens](https://bemamas.com/baby-and-child/starting-solids-allergens/) | Allergen introduction and related considerations | Brief signpost, with no duplicate allergen protocol |
| [Six-month milestones](https://bemamas.com/baby-and-child/six-month-milestones/) | Whole-child developmental overview | A focused feeding routine and a practical observation record |

### Original outline and useful visual

1. **Answer first: milk and first foods can coexist.** Explain the role of complementary foods around six months and the need for readiness. Avoid suggesting that the birthday alone makes a baby ready.
2. **Before using a schedule.** Brief readiness check and a link to the existing solids guide. Flag premature birth and individual feeding plans for professional advice.
3. **Build around your usual feeds and cues.** Describe choosing a calm opportunity to explore food. Do not force a universal milk-first/solids-first rule: national sources and individual needs can differ.
4. **One illustrative routine, two ways to read it.** A day-part table with milk feeds as responsive events and an opportunity for first foods. Do not specify a mandatory number of feeds or replace milk with solids on a schedule.
5. **Record observations, not a pass/fail score.** A printable table: time, nursing/bottle, bottle amount and unit if applicable, food/texture offered, observed cues, notes. Nursing minutes must never be converted into milk volume.
6. **Safety at the table.** Sourced guidance on readiness, texture and supervision; link out to appropriate allergy and choking guidance. Avoid unsourced emergency instructions.
7. **When the routine is not working.** Address refusal and variable appetite without dismissing illness or intake concerns. Any thresholds or urgent-care language require clinical review and primary citations.
8. **FAQ and next step.** Do solids replace milk? Must every day look the same? Can a tracking app decide how much my baby needs? Link to BeMama’s nursing and bottle workflows as recording aids.

**Visual specification:** Use a responsive day-part table and a printable observation worksheet in the current theme. Label the routine “example, not a feeding prescription.” Do not make an intake-target chart, nutrition score or percentile graph from unsupported data. Any future unit toggle must label the fluid-ounce convention and retain the original recorded unit.

### Claim-to-source requirements

| Proposed claim or boundary | Primary source and scope |
| --- | --- |
| Begin complementary foods around six months with readiness; consider individual advice for premature babies | [NHS first solid foods](https://www.nhs.uk/baby/weaning-and-feeding/babys-first-solid-foods/). UK guidance; localize recommendations deliberately rather than presenting every national detail as global. |
| Early solids accompany milk; breast milk or first infant formula remains the main drink during the first year | Same NHS source. An example routine must preserve this principle. |
| Readiness, appropriate food preparation/texture and allergen considerations | [CDC introducing solid foods](https://www.cdc.gov/infant-toddler-nutrition/foods-and-drinks/when-what-and-how-to-introduce-solid-foods.html). Obtain clinical review for high-risk allergy wording; do not invent a universal introduction schedule. |
| Bottle amount and nursing duration are different records; BeMama does not infer intake from nursing minutes | Implementation and limits in [feature-evidence.md](feature-evidence.md), C03–C04. |

Before publication, have an appropriate pediatric feeding reviewer check the routine, food preparation examples, refusal/help-seeking language and the handling of regional guidance. Do not publish fixed oz/mL targets or number-of-feeds requirements solely because a competitor uses them.
