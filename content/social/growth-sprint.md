# BeMama organic growth sprint

Goal: reach parents who can genuinely use BeMama without paid promotion, irrelevant friend invites, automated comments, or mass messaging.

## Daily three-part cadence

1. **Discovery post** — Reel or short video with a relatable parenting moment and one question.
2. **Helpful post** — carousel or single graphic with a small, practical takeaway. Add a short “general education, not medical advice” note when the topic is health-related.
3. **Conversation Story** — poll, question sticker, behind-the-scenes moment, or a reshare of a thoughtful community response with permission.

Keep each item useful on its own. Do not repeat the same caption three times in one day.

## Relevant audiences to find

Search for public accounts and communities connected to:

- postpartum support, new-parent support, and local parent groups
- lactation consultants, breastfeeding educators, and pumping-at-work support
- doulas, midwives, prenatal educators, and childbirth classes
- baby sleep educators and realistic newborn-routine creators
- combination-feeding, bottle-feeding, and formula-feeding support
- pelvic-floor physiotherapy, maternal mental health, and parent wellness
- family-friendly libraries, community centres, and early-years programs

Use the location plus the topic when looking for local accounts. Follow only accounts that are genuinely relevant to BeMama’s audience, and read their rules before participating.

## Safe daily outreach routine

- Leave 3–5 thoughtful comments on relevant public posts. Each comment should add a useful observation or ask a sincere question; never paste the same comment.
- Reply to every genuine comment on BeMama’s posts within a reasonable window.
- Save 2–3 potential collaborators per week: small educators, doulas, lactation professionals, parent creators, or local family organizations whose audiences overlap.
- Send at most 1–2 personalized collaboration messages per week, only after interacting naturally with the account.
- Do not invite unrelated friends, scrape followers, follow/unfollow repeatedly, or send promotional links to strangers.

## Comment starters

Adapt these to the specific post; do not use them verbatim every time:

- “The reminder that routines need to fit the family—not the other way around—is so helpful.”
- “This is a useful way to make the next step feel manageable. Which part do parents ask you about most?”
- “I appreciate the non-judgmental framing. Feeding plans can change a lot in the first year.”
- “This would make a great checklist for a tired caregiver. What is the one item people most often forget?”

## Collaboration message

> Hi [name] — I’ve been following your practical, non-judgmental support for [specific audience]. I run BeMama, a planning and support app for pregnancy, baby, and parenting routines. I liked your post about [specific post]. Would you be open to a small educational collaboration, such as a joint Q&A or a short checklist? No sales pitch—just useful support for your audience. Happy to follow your format and community rules.

## Content themes with discovery potential

Rotate relatable moments with useful education:

- the invisible work of feeding, pumping, cleaning, and remembering supplies
- returning to work while protecting time, privacy, and rest
- breastfeeding, bottle-feeding, formula, and combination feeding without scorekeeping
- responsive feeding and hunger/fullness cues
- starting solids without internet pressure or unsafe shortcuts
- newborn sleep expectations and the tiny wins that count
- postpartum mental load, support handoffs, and asking for help

Avoid sensational medical claims, guaranteed outcomes, target milk volumes, miracle hacks, or advice that replaces a clinician.

## Simple weekly review

Track the post URL, reach, saves, shares, profile visits, follows, and meaningful comments. After seven days, keep the topics that earn saves, shares, and real questions—not only likes. Repeat winning themes with a new example instead of reposting the same creative.
